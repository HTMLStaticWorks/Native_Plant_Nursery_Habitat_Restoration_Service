Choose the Right Native Plants

Match plants to your specific site conditions for ecological success.

Full Sun (6+ hrs)

Sun Loving

Requires 6+ hours of uninterrupted direct sunlight. Perfect for establishing resilient tallgrass prairie, sunny meadows, and open borders.

Top Species: Purple Coneflower, Little Bluestem, Black-Eyed Susan
Partial to Full Shade

Shade Tolerant

Thrives under dense tree canopies with under 4 hours of dappled light. Ideal for rich woodland understories, north-facing slopes, and fern glades.

Top Species: White Trillium, Maidenhair Fern, Wild Ginger, Foamflower
Xeric & Well-Drained

Dry Soil

Deep-rooted, drought-tolerant species that thrive in sandy, gravelly, or shallow soils without requiring supplemental summer irrigation once established.

Top Species: Butterfly Weed (*Asclepias tuberosa*), Stonecrop, Sideoats Grama
Hydric & Rain Gardens

Moist Soil

Adapted to periodic flooding and damp soils. Excellent choices for engineered bio-swales, rain gardens, pond fringes, and seasonal retention basins.

Top Species: Swamp Milkweed, Blue Flag Iris, Cardinal Flower, Joe Pye Weed
Bees & Butterflies

Pollinator Gardens

High-nectar and pollen-yielding flowers engineered to bloom in continuous succession from April to October to sustain native solitary bees and butterflies.

Top Species: Wild Bergamot (*Monarda*), Common Milkweed, Stiff Goldenrod
Nesting & Forage

Bird Habitat

Architectural shrubs and keystone canopy trees offering winter berries, protective nesting structures, and crucial host plants for caterpillar foraging.

Top Species: Serviceberry (*Amelanchier*), American Elderberry, White Oak

Step-by-Step Planting Guide

Follow these steps to give your native plants the best start.

1

Prepare the Site

Remove invasive species and lawn grass. Do not till the soil, as this brings dormant weed seeds to the surface.

2

Select Species

Choose plants appropriate for your light and soil conditions. Avoid amending the soil; native plants prefer native soil.

3

Dig the Hole

Dig a hole twice as wide as the pot but no deeper. The root flare should sit slightly above ground level.

4

Plant Correctly

Gently loosen the roots. Place in hole and backfill with the original soil. Tamp down lightly to remove air pockets.

5

Water Properly

Water deeply immediately after planting. Continue watering during dry spells for the first year until established.

6

Maintain & Monitor

Mulch lightly with natural leaf litter or hardwood mulch (keep away from stems). Pull weeds as needed.

Seasonal Planting Calendar

When to plant, when to seed, and when to plan for optimal restoration success.

Spring March – May

Prime window for planting container stock, plugs, and bare-root shrubs as soil awakens. Ensure danger of severe frost has passed. Early spring is also ideal for sowing warm-season grasses and establishing root networks before summer heat.

Summer June – August

Focus on hydration and site prep rather than new plantings. If you must plant, rigorous morning watering is mandatory. Ideal period for clearing invasive species, solarizing soil, and drafting restoration layouts for fall installation.

Fall September – November

Widely regarded as the ecological sweet spot! Cooler ambient temperatures reduce plant transpiration while soil remains warm enough to foster vigorous root establishment before winter dormancy. Perfect for planting trees, shrubs, and dormant seeding.

Winter December – February

The essential season for "winter sowing" and frost-seeding native prairie seeds onto frozen ground or snow cover. Natural freeze-thaw cycles break seed dormancy via cold-stratification. Also the best time for structural pruning of dormant native trees.
